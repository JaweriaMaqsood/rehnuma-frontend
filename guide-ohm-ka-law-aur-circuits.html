Ohm's law describes the relationship between current, voltage and resistance, and most electricity numericals are based on it.Ohm ka law current, voltage aur resistance ke rishte ko batata hai aur electricity ke zyadatar numericals isi par chalte hain.

Ohm's Law

V = I × R, that is I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I.V = I × R, yani I = V ÷ R aur R = V ÷ I.

  • V: voltage (volt, V)
  • I: current (ampere, A)
  • R: resistance (ohm, Ω)

This law applies to conductors whose temperature does not change (ohmic conductors).Ye law un conductors par lagta hai jin ka temperature nahi badalta (ohmic conductors).

Solved numericalHal kiya hua numerical

Question: A 12 V battery is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. How much current flows?Sawal: 12 V ki battery 4 Ω ke resistor se judi hai. Current kitni hogi?

Solution: I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.Hal: I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.

Series circuit

When resistors are connected one after another, R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃. The current is the same everywhere.Resistors ek ke baad ek lage hon to R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃. Current har jagah barabar rehti hai.

Example: 2 Ω and 3 Ω in series = 5 Ω.Misal: 2 Ω aur 3 Ω series mein = 5 Ω.

Parallel circuit

When resistors are on separate paths, 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂. The voltage is the same across every branch.Resistors alag alag raston par lage hon to 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂. Voltage har branch par barabar hota hai.

Example: 6 Ω and 3 Ω in parallel: 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2, so R = 2 Ω.Misal: 6 Ω aur 3 Ω parallel: 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2, yani R = 2 Ω.

Electric power

P = V × I (watt). From Ohm's law you also get P = I²R and P = V²/R.P = V × I (watt). Ohm ke law se P = I²R aur P = V²/R bhi milta hai.
